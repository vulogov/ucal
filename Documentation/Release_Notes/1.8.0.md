# 1.8.0 — 2026-08-24

The frozen four, measured.

Four limitations were frozen by 1.0 and carried unchanged in every release note
since, under a sentence that read like a schedule: *"whenever the work is done."*
Five cycles later nobody had counted the work. This cycle counted it, and the
count changed what two of the four are.

It also made two documented extension points real, closed four carried-forward
lines, and found that **the project's own verification block did not work if you
followed it** — CI had been insulated from that by running its halves on separate
machines.

**No 2.0 is cut.** The decision needs a number; the number now exists.

## The goal of this cycle: the frozen four, measured

Four limitations were frozen by 1.0 and have been carried, unchanged, in every
release note since. [`ROAD-TO-2.0.md`](../Proposals/ROAD-TO-2.0.md) decided what
happens to each of them and then said when:

> **When the bundle is worth a release, which is a question about the diff and
> not about the calendar.** [...] What is not yet decided is whether it is
> *urgent*, and the honest answer is no [...] That is not a gate. It is the
> observation that the release can be made whenever the work is done, and that
> nothing is made worse by doing the work in a cycle that has room for it.

Five cycles have passed and nobody has looked at the diff. "Whenever the work is
done" is not a schedule; it is the same shape as a claim with no mechanism, and
this project has spent three cycles finding out what those look like.

**This cycle measures it.** Not to ship 2.0 — that is a decision, and it comes
after the number, not before.

### What has changed since the decision

Two of the four have grown evidence while sitting frozen.

**`--all-features` cannot build this workspace**, because it enables both
backends and the guard fires. Three separate tools now want something the feature
surface cannot give them: `cargo semver-checks` needs `--default-features`, the
MSRV job needs `--features tui` spelled out, and 1.6.0's own audit had to write
around it. That is item 1 arriving from the outside, three times.

**Two documented extension points admit nothing new.** X3 found that `Bridge` and
`DeclaredTables` are `#[non_exhaustive]` with no constructor, so a downstream
`Profile` must borrow a bridge and a downstream `LegacyCalendar` must borrow its
tables. §8.6 says legacy calendars are "preserved for interoperation"; in
practice no calendar this crate does not already ship can be one. That is a fifth
item, found after the four were frozen.

### Scope

**A1 — make the extension points real, without breaking anything.**
Constructors for `Bridge` and `DeclaredTables`. Adding an inherent `new` to a
`#[non_exhaustive]` struct is additive, so this is a `1.x` change, and it turns
two documented extension points from decorative into usable.

**Stop if** a constructor cannot be given a signature that keeps the type's
invariants — `Bridge` carries a divisibility that must match its tick count, and
a constructor that let those disagree would be worse than no constructor. Then
the finding is that the type wants a builder or a checked constructor returning
`Result`, and that is still additive.

**A2 — measure the 2.0 diff.** For each of the four: how many public items
change, how many call sites, what a downstream crate would have to edit. The
deliverable is a number per item and a total, not a plan.

**Stop if** the measurement shows the bundle is smaller than expected — under a
few dozen sites — in which case the interesting question becomes why it was
deferred five times, and that belongs in the write-up.

**A3 — the second blind spot, if there is one.** X3 closed the one failure V1
measured in `cargo semver-checks` and said plainly what it did not:

> Whether there is a second exception is unmeasured, and finding out would mean
> building the same kind of external probe for every category of breaking
> change.

A2's number is only as good as the tool that produces it. Probe the categories a
2.0 would actually touch — a removed feature, a changed field type, a widened
enum — with the external-crate method that worked for traits.

**Stop if** the probes find nothing. That is the good outcome and it is worth one
paragraph, not a proposal.

### Done when

The four have a measured diff, the two extension points have constructors or a
recorded reason they cannot, and `semver-checks` has been probed in the
categories a 2.0 would touch.

**All three landed.** A1 gave four constructors — two more than planned, because
the first two were decorative without them. A2 measured the bundle by performing
the migration and reverting it. A3 found no second blind spot, so A2's number can
be relied on.

**No 2.0 is cut this cycle.** The decision needs the number; the number is what
this cycle produced.

### Explicitly not in scope

- **Anything breaking.** `cargo semver-checks` runs against the published 1.7.0,
  with the caveat A3 exists to measure.
- **Z2's remaining wall-clock items** — several dials, `--tier`, `--since`.
- **W3, the third locale**, still postponed pending a choice of language.

## Breaking

_none, and none is permitted._

## Added

- **A `full` feature** (B2): every feature this binary can have, on one backend.
  `--all-features` is the name a tool reaches for and cannot work here — it
  enables both integer backends and the guard fires — and three tools had wanted
  something the surface could not give them. It deliberately excludes `bigint`: a
  feature meaning "everything" that quietly picked a backend would be worse than
  no feature at all.
- **Constructors for the types a downstream extension point needs** (A1):
  `Bridge::new`, `DeclaredTables::new`, `DeclaredLeapRule::new`,
  `Discontinuity::new`. All additive.

  `Bridge::new` **derives** its divisibility rather than accepting it — §2.4's
  alignment invariants and D-3's exact decimal subdivisions both rest on it being
  the largest `n` with `5^n | ticks`, and a caller has no way to be told theirs is
  wrong. The other three return `Result`, because their obligations *can* be
  stated: months that make a common year, a week with days in it, a cycle with
  years in it, a discontinuity that says what it was — and, the one that matters,
  **§8.6's requirement that a legacy calendar declare what about it is
  arbitrary.** A calendar that declares nothing arbitrary is claiming to be
  derived, and it is not.

## Changed

- **`cargo semver-checks` has been probed in the categories a 2.0 would touch**
  (A3). Six categories — a removed feature, a removed derive, a changed field
  type, an added argument, and a variant added to each of a `#[non_exhaustive]`
  and a closed enum — each compared against what an actual downstream crate does.
  **Six agreements, no second blind spot.** The one known failure remains
  required trait methods, covered by X3's fixtures.
- **[`ROAD-TO-2.0.md`](../Proposals/ROAD-TO-2.0.md) has a number** (A2). The
  bundle was measured by performing the migration far enough to count and then
  reverting it. It is smaller than five cycles of deferral implied, and the
  headline is not what anyone expected: `Citation::new` need not change at all —
  it can keep taking `&'static str` and keep being `const fn`, because
  `Cow::Borrowed` is a const constructor — so all 29 call sites compile
  unchanged. **What actually breaks is `Copy`**, on seven types, and that lands
  on callers rather than on this workspace.

## Fixed

- **The body-file and anchor-file loaders interned** (B1). They leaked once per
  *call*; they now leak once per **distinct string**. A caller loading one file a
  thousand times costs what loading it once costs, which was the sharp form of
  [D-A20](../../spec/SPEC-DELTAS.md)'s objection — *"a caller loading in a loop
  leaks without bound"*. Asserted by pointer identity, not by description.

  **D-A20 does not move**, and the reason is on the delta: interning changes the
  shape of the leak, not its existence. A thousand *different* files still
  accumulate, and a loaded `Body` still cannot be dropped, because the data model
  is `&'static str` and that is the actual obstacle. What this removes is the
  case that was unbounded in a loop; what it leaves is the case that is unbounded
  in the corpus.

## Internal

- Workspace and all seven packages at `1.8.0`.
- **The documented verification block did not work if you followed it** (B-fix).
  Step 4 rebuilds `target/release/ucal` without `tui`, and steps 6 and 7 run that
  binary — so the block failed on its own last step. CI never saw it, because its
  `checks` job builds the binary itself and never runs the workspace tests. The
  block now rebuilds before the `xtask` line, and the CI-covers-procedure check
  holds the two together.
- **`verify-vectors` joined the defect corpus** (B6). X1 listed it as
  hand-verified for want of a root parameter — it read `workspace_root()` and
  could only ever run against the real tree, which left it the one `xtask` check
  with no recorded mutation. **22 mutations now, no survivors.**
- **[`GE-U4-walk.sh`](../Proposals/GE-U4-walk.sh) has been run** (B7). Carried as
  "built and unrun" in six release notes; forty-five frames, T-12 to T32, exit 0.
  It settles that the script works and that nothing drifted from
  `ucal ladder --json` across four releases. It does not settle the hypothesis,
  which needs two readers the author cannot be — so the carried line becomes
  "built, run, and waiting on two readers".
- **[`ROAD-TO-2.0.md`](../Proposals/ROAD-TO-2.0.md) carries A2's numbers** (B5),
  including a correction to its own text: it said item 2's cost to a caller was
  that "every construction site changes", and that is false.
- **The defect corpus no longer goes stale at a version bump.** Three entries
  named the version literally and reported `UNAPP` on this branch's first run,
  testing nothing. The verdict caught it, which is the design working; not
  needing to be caught is better, so the corpus substitutes `{VERSION}` now.

## Verification

```
cargo test --workspace --release
cargo test --workspace --exclude xtask --release --no-default-features \
  --features bigint,std,ucal-civil/hifitime,ucal/civil,ucal/body,ucal/events,ucal/cosmo
cargo test -p ucal --release --features tui
RUSTFLAGS="-D warnings" cargo build --workspace --all-targets --release
cargo build -p ucal-core --target wasm32-unknown-unknown \
  --no-default-features --features u512
# Rebuild the binary the documentation is generated from. The `--all-targets`
# build above replaces target/release/ucal with one that has no `tui`, and
# check-docs and corpus both run it. Without this line the block fails on its
# own last step, which it did until 1.8.0.
cargo build --release --locked -p ucal --features full
cargo run -p xtask && cargo run -p xtask -- lint \
  && cargo run -p xtask -- check-docs && cargo run -p xtask -- corpus \
  && cargo run -p xtask -- verify-vectors
```

At the close: **609 tests** on the default backend, **221** in `ucal` built with
`--features full`, **560** on bigint reaching the same Rule W digest over ten
thousand generated inputs, **22 corpus mutations with no survivors** — every
mechanisable check now has a recorded defect it must reject — `-D warnings` clean
across all targets with and without `full`, wasm `no_std` green, the UC-P0
constants harness meeting its exit criterion on both routes and refusing to meet
it below sixty checks, lints clean across **77 files** and each held to a floor,
nine `check-docs` checks passing, **25 spec deltas recorded and 24 standing and
applied** to the normative text, **136 citations** resolving across source *and*
documentation, fourteen cited URLs reaching their documents, MSRV 1.88 verified
on the workspace and on `--features full`, **22 feature combinations** building,
and all six crates clean under `cargo semver-checks` against the published 1.7.0
— probed in A3 for the categories a 2.0 would touch.

At the opening it was 596 tests, 21 mutations and 75 files.

## What this cycle found out about itself

**A measurement is not a grep.** Item 2 was assumed for five cycles to be a large
mechanical rename — thirty-four construction sites. Performing it found that
`Citation::new` need not change at all, and that what actually breaks is `Copy`,
on seven types, landing on callers rather than here. Two of those seven were only
found when the compiler reached them; the first count, taken by grep, was wrong.

**An extension point is only as usable as the least constructible type in its
signature.** A1's first `DeclaredTables::new` took two arguments no outsider
could build. It compiled, read well, and was useless to the only caller it
existed for — found by writing the test, not by reading the code.

**A schedule with no date is the third of a set.** This project has now found a
claim with no mechanism (1.5.0), a mechanism with no wire (1.6.0), and a schedule
nobody was asked to keep (1.8.0). `ROAD-TO-2.0.md` diagnosed the pattern about the
contact gate and reproduced it one section later.

**And a procedure whose steps undo each other.** The documented verification block
rebuilds the binary without `tui` in step 4 and runs it in steps 6 and 7, so it
failed on its own last step. CI never saw it, because its `checks` job builds the
binary itself and never runs the workspace tests. Both halves stayed green while
drifting apart, which is the 1.6.0 finding one level up.
