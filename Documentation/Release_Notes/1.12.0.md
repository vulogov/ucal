# 1.12.0 — UNRELEASED

In development on the `1.12.0` branch. `main` carries the released 1.11.0 line.

## The goal of this cycle: *released* is a claim about the world

1.9.0's theme was **the artefact, not the repository**. 1.11.0's was **the
standard that stops at the edge of one build**. This cycle is where those two
meet, and it opens with three instances already found — none of them by a check,
and all three within two days of each other.

Every one has the same structure: **the repository did the work, wrote down the
outcome, and never asked the world whether the outcome happened.**

### The three known instances

**1.10.0 was marked released and exists nowhere.** The Contents table below said
*released — 2026-08-28*. There is no tag in any remote, no GitHub release, and
no crate on `crates.io`, which goes 1.9.0 → 1.11.0. The procedure has ten steps
and the first seven ran; the table was written on the strength of the cut. Found
by reading which baseline `cargo semver-checks` had chosen — it fetches the
newest *published* version and fetched 1.9.0, while 1.11.0's notes said in two
places that the comparison was against 1.10.0.

**v1.9.0 is unsigned, and 1.9.0's notes say it is the first release whose
binaries a downloader can check end to end.** The mechanism was built and is
correct: `sign-release` exists, the key was published in five places, and
`verify-release` checks the signature. The command was never run for that tag.
`verify-release 1.9.0` has been reporting `UNSIGNED` the whole time.

So **v1.11.0 is the first signed release**, and 1.9.0's sentence — repeated in
1.10.0's and 1.11.0's *Carried forward* sections, which is three notes carrying
one unchecked claim — was never true. It is now unfixable for a reason the
project already published: the key of that era is dead, and signing with today's
key would say a 2026-08-31 key vouched for a 2026-08-27 artefact, which is
precisely the claim [`spec/CONFORMANCE.md`](../../spec/CONFORMANCE.md) refuses
to make about pre-1.9.0 releases.

**`verify-release` has two standing failures and nothing reads it.** For v1.9.0,
five crates differ in `Cargo.lock`. For v1.11.0, six differ in
`.cargo_vcs_info.json` — because 1.11.0 published from the cut commit while the
tag sits on the merge, which the procedure did not say to avoid until it had
happened. Every other byte matches in both cases. A check that is known to fail
trains its reader to ignore it, and *CI green with no known-failing job* was a
1.0 exit criterion.

### Scope

**R1 — a version marked released must exist where a reader can get it.** For
every row in the Contents table not marked unreleased: the tag resolves on the
remote, a GitHub release exists for it, and every crate is on the registry at
that version. Network-dependent, so it belongs on a schedule beside
`check-links` and not on `push` — a check that reddens the tree because
`crates.io` is having a bad morning trains its reader to ignore it, which is the
defect this item is about.

**Stop if** it cannot distinguish *not published yet* from *published and gone*.
A release in progress must not be a failure, and a check that cannot tell those
apart would have to be run by hand at exactly the moment nobody is watching.

**R2 — `verify-release`'s standing failures are explained or fixed.** Two
different causes and probably two different answers. `.cargo_vcs_info.json` is
settled going forward and unfixable for 1.11.0, so it wants an enumerated
exception with its reason — the shape the retired signing key got, not a
silenced comparison. `Cargo.lock` is not understood yet and is the one to
measure first.

**Stop if** the `Cargo.lock` difference turns out to be cargo resolving
differently today than at publish time. Then the finding is that *`cargo package`
is byte-reproducible* — measured across two releases and stated in 1.9.0's
notes — was overclaimed, and the claim needs narrowing rather than the check
needing fixing.

**R3 — what makes a command reach the `ucal-json/1` promise.** Two
hand-maintained lists enumerate the same commands: `commands()` in
`json_surface.rs` and `all_commands()` in `manual_fields.rs`. Nothing checks
either is complete. `the_baseline_covers_every_command` checks that every
*listed* command contributes, which is the weaker half of the question.

1.11.0 found this the only way it can currently be found: `add --in` was added to
one list, and the *other* file failed, reporting a documented field no command
emitted. Seven commands are absent from the baseline today and every one of them
is legitimately absent — `cal`, `cosmo` and `events` are parents whose
subcommands are listed; `completions`, `man` and `help` emit roff and shell
rather than documents; `seq` emits bare lines under `--json` deliberately.
**Nothing establishes that.** A legitimate absence and an oversight are the same
thing to every check in the tree.

**Stop if** enumerating the commands mechanically needs the `clap` tree, which
these tests do not have and cannot get without shelling out to the binary — the
reason `manual_fields` is a test and not a lint. Then the answer is an explicit
list of *deliberately absent* commands with a reason each, which is worse than a
derivation and much better than nothing.

### Done when

Every claim this repository makes about a released artefact is either checked
against the world, or written down as a claim nobody checks.

### Explicitly not in scope

- **Anything breaking.** `cargo semver-checks` runs against the published 1.11.0.
- **Signing v1.9.0 retrospectively.** Refused for the reason above, which is the
  project's own published reasoning rather than a new one.
- **The 2.0 decision.** [`ROAD-TO-2.0.md`](../Proposals/ROAD-TO-2.0.md) carries a
  measured diff and five items; the go/no-go is the author's.
- **H1's fourth finding.** The satellite set has no time coordinate, and
  `Body::formation()` is declared and read by nothing.
- **W3, the third locale**, still waiting on a choice of language.
- **The cycle's new feature is not chosen yet.**
  [`S1`](../Proposals/S1-astrophysics-roadmap.md) proposes a sequence of them
  for astrophysics across several cycles, assesses `siderust` and rejects it as

  not become an ephemeris library**. Its first item, JD/MJD conversion with the

## Added

- **`cargo run -p xtask -- check-releases`** (R1) — every version the Contents
  table marks *released* has a tag on the remote, a GitHub release, and all six
  crates on the registry.

  **1.10.0's row said `released` and 1.10.0 existed nowhere.** Nothing noticed
  for three days and nothing would have, because every other check in this tree
  reads files that are in it. *Released* is a claim about the world.

  **Scheduled daily, not on `push`** — `links.yml`'s reasoning, and the schedule
  is tighter for a reason it does not share: a rotted URL rots on nobody's
  timetable, while this one is wrong precisely in the hours after a release,
  which is when somebody is still around to finish the job. A failure opens an
  issue and a later one comments on it; the run is where the detail lives.

  Three things are enumerated rather than inferred. The **state vocabulary** is
  closed, so a row whose state nothing recognises is a failure and not a row
  quietly skipped — `cut, never published` is 1.10.0's and cannot become the
  escape hatch every awkward release takes. **`gh` failing to authenticate is a
  refusal**, because an unauthenticated `gh` reports no releases at all and that
  is indistinguishable from a repository with none. And the **GitHub-release
  floor is 1.0.0**, measured: of the 0.x line only `v0.6.0` has one, so
  demanding them of `v0.1.0` would fill the output with history and a check
  whose output is known-bad entries is one nobody reads.

  It deliberately does not check that the artefact is the *right* one.
  `verify-release` is that check.

## Fixed

- **`verify-release` has no standing failures** (R2). It had two, from the day
  each version was published, and nothing read either.

  **v1.9.0: five crates differed in `Cargo.lock`, and the cause is a claim with
  a shelf life.** `cargo package` strips the `path` from an intra-workspace
  dependency and resolves the registry requirement instead, so packaging
  `ucal-civil` records whichever `ucal-core` satisfies `^1.9.0` *at that moment*.
  In August that was 1.9.0, because nothing newer existed; today it is 1.11.0.
  Four lines differ, all one `[[package]]` block, and `ucal-core` still matches
  to the byte because it is the one crate with no sibling to resolve.

  So **`cargo package` is not reproducible for this workspace** from the moment
  a later compatible version is published. 1.9.0's notes recorded
  byte-reproducibility measured across 1.8.0; the measurement was right and
  nobody stated that it expires. The comparison now normalises exactly those
  entries and compares everything else verbatim — a third-party version moving,
  or a sibling's *dependency list* changing, still fails, and four tests say so.

  **v1.11.0: six crates differed in `.cargo_vcs_info.json`**, because it
  published from the cut commit while the tag sits on the merge. Enumerated
  against that one version with its reason, the shape the retired signing key
  got — a blanket exemption would leave the next release free to repeat it
  unnoticed. A test asserts every entry names a single version and carries a
  reason.

  The reason prints **once**, as a legend, rather than on each of six lines: a
  paragraph repeated until it is scenery is how a standing failure goes unread.

- **A command reaches the `ucal-json/1` promise by more than being remembered**
  (R3), and the check found one that had not.

  Two hand-maintained lists enumerate the same commands — `commands()` in
  `json_surface.rs` and `all_commands()` in `manual_fields.rs`. The existing
  `the_baseline_covers_every_command` asserts every *listed* command
  contributes, which is the weaker half. Nothing asked whether the list was
  complete, and 1.11.0 found that out the only way then available: `add --in`
  was added to one list and the *other* file failed.

  `check-docs`'s eleventh check reads the commands from `main.rs` — subcommands
  included, because `cal export` arrived in 1.10.0 and a check stopping at the
  top-level `cal` would have had nothing to say about it. Each must contribute
  to the surface baseline or be listed as emitting no document, **with a
  reason**.

  **On its first run it found `cal export`**, which emits an HJSON body file
  because a generator's output is an input to something else. Legitimately
  absent — and until now a legitimate absence and an oversight looked identical
  to every check in the tree. `CLI.md` now says so, as `seq`, `man` and
  `completions` already did.

  Checked in both directions: an entry for a command that does not exist fails,
  and so does one for a command that turns out to contribute. The corpus gained
  the mutation that proves the second — **24 mutations, none surviving**.

## Carried forward from 1.11.0

- **The signing key was rotated on 2026-08-31**, because the passphrase to
  `D0E4E5A9439E54CC` was lost. It carries no passphrase, and both keys are
  quoted here because naming an ID a reader cannot check is worse than saying
  nothing — the rule 0.5.0 set, and the one `check-docs` enforced against the
  first draft of this file:

  ```
  current   EBD5E4F1EBA555E0   RWTgVaXr8eTV6+dsVwvMkwZglwUJS69tF+78i2MFUi5LBaUXPf66M+FV
  retired   D0E4E5A9439E54CC   RWTMVJ5DqeXk0HgeN+BIdnQaamRTdzkjITkdprOPLVsGWP8R/2HYIj0r
  ```
 The custody plan's offline backup guarded the machine and not the
  secret that opened it — a copy of the same encrypted file — which is 1.11.0's
  own theme, in the custody statement, unlooked for.
- **Nothing signs the new key**, because the key that could have is the one that
  was lost. It arrives announced by the same repository whose contents a
  rotation exists to let you doubt, and a reader who trusted the old key has no
  cryptographic path to the new one.
- **The immutable copies disagree, permanently.** Every crate published at
  1.11.0 or earlier carries the retired key on `crates.io` and cannot be edited,
  so the tamper-evidence reports a real discrepancy with a benign cause and
  cannot tell it from the case it was built for. Only a version published after
  the rotation restores the comparison — which makes it 1.12.0's job.
- **v1.11.0 published from the cut commit rather than the merge**, so
  `.cargo_vcs_info.json` names a commit a checkout of the tag cannot reproduce.
  The procedure now says to merge first; the published crates cannot be
  replaced.
- **`-D warnings` reaches all 23 feature combinations**, and the six items it
  found were every one of them in the binary crate.
- **Both of the book's generators are checked.** One had drifted twenty lines,
  and its own header says it exists because hand-copied lists drift silently.
- **`xtask` is outside the lint on purpose**, and the reason is now recorded
  where the scoping happens rather than nowhere.
- **`add --in` moves by a date where `--step` moves by a duration**, and refuses
  a day the destination local year does not have rather than clamping to one
  nobody asked for.
- **Two of fifteen calendars have an anchor.** That is the ceiling on local
  fields, on `cal from`, and on `add --in`.
- **§15.1 is `UNIMPLEMENTED`** for `ucal-body` (D-A20).
