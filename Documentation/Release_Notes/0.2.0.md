# 0.2.0 — UNRELEASED

In development on the `0.2.0` branch. `main` carries the released 0.1.1 line.

Nothing is published at this version. Entries below accumulate as work lands;
add to them in the same commit as the change, not on release day.

## The goal of this cycle: supersede RFC UCAL-1

**Bring the specification into the repository, corrected, so that the source
tree is the normative record and the external RFC becomes a historical
artefact.**

### Why this, and why now

The implementation already outranks the RFC on what the system *does*: 376
tests, two independent integer backends, and `xtask` re-deriving every
Appendix A constant along two routes. It also outranks it on what is *true*.
Fourteen standing deltas are recorded in
[`spec/SPEC-DELTAS.md`](../../spec/SPEC-DELTAS.md), and the RFC text is
deliberately unchanged, so a reader following the RFC alone today gets fourteen
wrong answers — including a synodic formula that computes the wrong quantity
(D-A12) and an integral that cannot be quadratured as written (D-A14).

But the RFC **is not in this repository**, and the source cites it constantly:

| citation form | occurrences | distinct |
|---|---:|---:|
| sections, `§N.N` | 494 | 75 |
| rules, `Rule X` | 538 | 24 |
| decisions, `D-N` | — | 17 |
| failure modes, `FN` | — | 10 |
| appendices | — | 9 |

Retiring the RFC in that state would not promote the source to source of truth.
It would orphan a thousand references and make the codebase's own reasoning
unreadable — every `§10.6`, `Rule X`, `F8` and `D-12` in a doc comment becomes
a pointer to nothing.

So: **supersede, do not retire.** The distinction is the whole scope of this
work.

### Scope

Five pieces, in dependency order. The first is a blocker for the rest.

**S1 — Vendor the RFC. _(blocker)_**
`spec/RFC-UCAL-1.md`, verbatim, ~89 000 characters / ~1 300 lines as issued,
marked historical and immutable at the top. Nothing else can proceed while the
deltas have no text to be deltas *against*. Mechanical; the only judgement is
the header wording.

**S2 — A rules glossary.**
`spec/RULES.md`. All 24 rules are cited 538 times across the codebase and
defined nowhere in it. One paragraph each: what it requires, which failure mode
it guards, and where it is enforced — type system, test, or lint. The most-cited
are `J` (62), `K` (59), `Q` (50), `N` (34), `R` (33), `Y` (31), `T` (29).

Highest value per unit of work in the whole cycle: it makes 538 citations
self-resolving, and it is the piece a newcomer needs first.

**S3 — Fold the deltas into a corrected text.**
`spec/UCAL-1.1.md`: the vendored RFC with all fourteen standing deltas applied
in place, each amended passage marked with the delta that changed it. Three are
**AMENDMENT** (D-A5, D-A8, D-A9 — normative changes adopted by decision), six
are **CORRECTION** (D-A4, D-A7, D-A11, D-A12, D-A13, D-A14 — the RFC is wrong),
and the remaining five are **EDITORIAL**. D-A1 stays withdrawn and visible, because a
retracted claim is part of the record.

`SPEC-DELTAS.md` is then kept as *provenance* rather than as errata: it explains
why UCAL-1.1 differs from UCAL-1, which is exactly the question a reader of the
original will have.

The 75 cited sections must survive with their numbering intact — `§21.3` alone
is referenced 43 times — so this is an amendment of the text, never a
renumbering of it.

**S4 — Sign the conformance vectors.**
`fixtures/vectors.json` (27 KB: constants, provenance, alignment, tiers,
fixtures, continued fractions) plus `SHA256SUMS` already exist and regenerate
from `xtask`. §20 asks for a *signed* vector file; `xtask` correctly reports
that detached signing needs a key and is a release step, not a harness step.
Unsigned vectors are a sound internal check and not something a third-party
implementer can trust. Needs a key and a documented verification procedure.

**S5 — Close the loop.**
Point every in-repo reference at `spec/UCAL-1.1.md`, extend
`xtask -- check-docs` to fail when a cited section does not exist in the
vendored text, and state the supersession in `README.md`.

The `check-docs` extension is what makes this durable rather than a one-time
tidy: it turns "the citations resolve" from a claim into something CI enforces,
the same way §13.5's generated tier table cannot drift today.

### Done when

- A reader who has never seen the original RFC can work from this repository
  alone.
- Every `§`, `Rule`, `D-` and `F` citation in the source resolves to text in
  `spec/`, checked mechanically.
- `spec/UCAL-1.1.md` is internally consistent with the implementation, and
  where it is not, the difference is a recorded delta rather than a surprise.
- The conformance vectors are signed, and verifying them is documented.

### Explicitly not in scope

- **Rewriting the RFC's prose.** Supersession corrects what is wrong and
  changes nothing that is merely differently worded. The original's voice is
  part of the record.
- **Renumbering sections.** 494 citations depend on the current numbering.
- **A UC-2 profile.** GE-6's kill criterion fired and D-21 stands; that
  decision is settled and is not reopened by this work.

## Carried forward from 0.1.x

Open items, none of them regressions. Recorded here so the cycle starts with
them visible rather than rediscovered.

- **`[workspace.dependencies]` for internal crates.** Twelve version
  requirements are twelve edits per bump. Moving them makes it one.
- **`ucal-cosmo` quadrature converges at O(h).** The interval extension is
  first-order, so halving the width costs twice the panels. A mean-value form
  would be O(h²), but it needs a *signed* rational interval type, since the
  derivative's bound goes negative and `Ratio` is non-negative by construction
  (Rule Z applied to rationals). Worth doing only if the arithmetic width ever
  becomes the binding constraint — at the default depth it is 40× smaller than
  the parameter width, so today it is not.
- **Titan has no anchor** and will not get one by invention. It needs a
  published convention to cite, or it stays structurally complete and unusable
  for dating (GE-3).
- **`z_of_t` bisection budget.** Sixty-four halvings of `[0, 10000]` resolve `z`
  to ~5e-16, which is still tens of seconds of cosmic time. A one-tick
  tolerance returns `UCAL-E0071`; that is honest, and it is also a ceiling
  nobody has tried to raise.
