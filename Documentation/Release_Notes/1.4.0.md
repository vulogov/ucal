# 1.4.0 — UNRELEASED

In development on the `1.4.0` branch. `main` carries the released 1.3.0 line.

## The goal of this cycle: decide 2.0 before it is forced

Three limitations were frozen by 1.0 and have been carried forward, unchanged,
in every release note since:

- **The backends are mutually exclusive**, so two libraries that each chose one
  cannot appear in a single dependency graph. Demonstrated in 0.9.0, not
  asserted: two crates, one on each backend, and anything depending on both
  fails to build. Neither library author can fix it.
- **The no-panic guarantee covers the binary, not the libraries.** A caller
  linking `ucal-core` gets neither the lint nor the handler, and those crates
  carry twenty-two `expect` calls on established invariants.
- **`Measured` cannot express a retrograde rotation.** Venus is stored as a
  magnitude with the sign in a comment, and the synodic formula run with that
  magnitude is wrong by a factor of twenty-five.

Each needs a breaking change. Each has been "2.0's" for four cycles. **There is
no plan for 2.0**, and `ROAD-TO-1.0.md` — a document this project spent five
cycles following — ends at 1.0.

That is the same shape as the thirty-nine undecided types 0.6.0 existed to
settle: a decision nobody has made, accumulating, which will be made under
pressure or by default if it is not made deliberately. The difference is that
this time there is no deadline, which makes it easier to keep not doing.

### What this cycle is not

**Not doing the breaking work.** 1.4.0 is a minor release and
`cargo semver-checks` runs against the published 1.3.0. Designing a change and
making it are different acts, and the point of separating them is that a design
written under no pressure can still be rejected.

**Not committing to 2.0 happening.** The most likely outcome remains that `1.x`
is where this project stops, and the plan must say what that means for the three
items rather than assuming a major release will arrive to absorb them.

### Scope

**W1 — `ROAD-TO-2.0.md`. _(done)_** For each of the three: what the fix actually is, what
it costs a caller, and — the part that decides it — whether the limitation is
worth a major version at all. A major release is not free: it splits the
ecosystem, and this one has no users to split, which cuts both ways.

It also needs the condition under which 2.0 *never happens*.

**Written without a contact gate, and that changed during the cycle.** The first
draft mirrored `ROAD-TO-1.0.md` and gated 2.0 on someone arriving. That was
rejected on the grounds that it repeats a known failure — see the entry under
*Added*.

**W2 — the event catalogue.** Eleven entries for "the whole of time on one
screen", and `ucal timeline` is one of the three things `CONTACT.md` asks a
stranger to look at. Additive, cited, and the kind of work that makes the
timeline show something rather than demonstrate that it could.

**W3 — a third locale. _Postponed._** `LocaleId` ships `en` and `ru`. Rule N
says names are display-only and the canonical identity of a tier is its
exponent; a third locale would be the cheapest demonstration that the claim is
structural rather than a convenience of having written the names in English
first.

Postponed rather than dropped, and the reason is that **the choice of language
decides what the probe tests**, which makes it a decision rather than a task —
see the note under *Done when*.

### Done when

**Met.** `ROAD-TO-2.0.md` exists and decides all four items — one of them
closed rather than deferred. W2 landed, X1 landed in four stages, W4 step 1 ran
and closed its own proposal, and **W3 is postponed, with the reason below.**

#### Why W3 was postponed

Not because it is hard. The code is five small edits and a ten-row table, and
`LocaleId` is `#[non_exhaustive]` so a variant breaks nothing.

**The work is Appendix D's criterion, not the translation.** Names must be
short, concrete motion words with no mythological, religious, national or
numeric-prefix content. `пролёт` is not a translation of *span*; it is an
independently chosen Russian word meeting the same criterion. A third locale
produced by dictionary lookup would demonstrate nothing, and choosing the
language is therefore the substantive act.

**And the choice decides what gets tested.** `every_short_form_is_detectably_not_latin`
holds every short form to containing a Cyrillic letter with no Latin homoglyph,
so `гл` cannot be mistaken for Latin text. That rule is **scripted, not
general**: a Latin-script locale cannot satisfy it at all, and a Greek or
Devanagari one would need its own list.

So a third locale in Greek forces a narrow question and one in Spanish forces
the broad one — *is "detectably not Latin" a property of Cyrillic short forms,
or of short forms generally?* The likely answer is that the general invariant is
already covered by `no_two_short_forms_collide_or_reverse_each_other`, and the
homoglyph rule belongs in the `ru` table's own documentation rather than being
applied to every locale. **That decision is most of W3's value**, and it is
recorded here so it survives the postponement.

**What a third locale would still not prove.** That the names are *good*.
Whether `пролёт` reads naturally to a Russian speaker is a question for a
Russian speaker, and this project has none — the same missing reader as GE-A4,
GE-U4 and W4. A third locale written by the author is a third set of names
nobody has read.

## Added

- **Eleven more catalogued events (W2)**, taking `ucal timeline` from eleven to
  twenty-two — and, for the first time, past the present.

  `bbn`, `matter-radiation-equality`, `dark-energy-domination`,
  `earth-formation`, `great-oxidation`, `permian-triassic`, `homo-sapiens`,
  `holocene`, and three predictions: `stelliferous-end`, `proton-decay` and
  `black-hole-evaporation`. Four new citations — Cyburt et al. 2016, Gumsley et
  al. 2017, Hublin et al. 2017, Adams & Laughlin 1997 — and `CITATION_SET`
  bumped, which is what D-7 made this a separate crate for.

  **The far-future three are the point.** Every event in the catalogue used to
  sit inside the first ten tiers: forty-five rungs existed and the upper half
  held nothing, so the ladder was a claim rather than a display.
  `black-hole-evaporation` now reaches **T31**, and it is still eleven tiers
  below the ceiling.

  They are labelled as predictions rather than observations, and `proton-decay`
  keeps its conditional in the entry itself — experiment has only ever set lower
  bounds, and a catalogue that stated it flatly would be asserting a theory.

- **`ucal cal derive <file>` and a §15.1 body-file loader (X1.3, X1.4)** — a
  body that does not ship can now be authored as a file and shown the calendar
  it derives, without editing this crate.
  [`Documentation/examples/europa.hjson`](../examples/europa.hjson) is one.

  **In the binary, not in `ucal-body`, and that boundary is the finding.** §15.1
  puts the loader in the library. Every string in the data model is a
  `&'static str` — `Citation::new` in `ucal-core`, `Body::new`, `Measured`'s
  verbatim, unit and quantity, thirty-four sites — so a runtime loader must
  either `Box::leak` or change a published type. Leaking is bounded and harmless
  in a process that exits and **unbounded in a library**, where a caller loading
  in a loop inherits it; owning the strings is a breaking change to `ucal-core`
  and therefore 2.0's. So the loader lives where the leak is bounded, §15.1
  stays `UNIMPLEMENTED` for the library, and D-A20 carries the reason. This is a
  partial implementation of a normative requirement and is described as one.

  **`UCAL-E0012` has its first caller.** The code — *unknown key in an HJSON
  data file* — was defined for this loader and had none anywhere in the
  workspace; the loader is strict, and an unknown key raises it.

  **Rule C is not optional in a file.** Value, unit, citation and validity
  window are required fields and a file omitting one is refused, because a
  format that let them be optional would be a second and laxer way of declaring
  a body.

- **A finding that changes how a body file must be written.** Titan's solar day
  is not published — `data::titan()` *derives* it exactly. Written as a file it
  has to be rounded, and six decimals differ from the exact derivation by
  **3.9 × 10⁻⁷ days**.

  The continued fraction of days-per-year diverges at the **fifth term** —
  `[1, 3, 35, 1, 1, 1, 5, 1]` becomes `[1, 3, 35, 1, 106, 6, 3, 1]` — and the
  chosen convergent changes with it. **A rounded parameter is a different
  calendar.**

  That is Rule K working rather than a defect: an intercalation rule is a
  continued fraction and continued fractions are violently sensitive to their
  inputs. But it means anyone authoring a body file decides their calendar's
  intercalation with the last digit they write, and it is recorded in `CLI.md`
  and pinned by a test rather than left to be discovered. The round-trip test
  uses Mars, whose solar day is measured, and reproduces `45/76` exactly.

- **[`ROAD-TO-2.0.md`](../Proposals/ROAD-TO-2.0.md) (W1)** — what happens to the
  limitations 1.0 froze. Four of them now, X1 having added one.

  **It has no contact gate, and the reason is the most useful thing in it.**
  `ROAD-TO-1.0.md` made contact the gate on 1.0; the gate was set, held for
  three cycles, and then overridden. That is a failed instrument. It produced no
  decision — it produced delay and then a reversal, which is worse than either
  shipping on schedule or holding firm, because the delay bought nothing and the
  reversal spent the credibility the gate was supposed to have.

  A gate on something that will not happen at a meaningful rate is not a
  decision procedure. This project's standard is that a claim needs a mechanism;
  a gate nobody will ever trip is a claim whose mechanism never runs. It reads
  as rigour and functions as deferral.

  The concern underneath it — *do not freeze a surface nobody has tested* — is
  kept. What changed is the instrument: "tested by a user" is the wrong proxy
  when there are no users, and five cycles have gone into better ones. Those
  answer *is this self-consistent*, which is answerable; they do not answer *is
  this right*, and neither did the gate.

  So each item is decided on one question: **does it cause a defect, and is the
  defect reachable?** Backend exclusivity: yes, a build failure neither library
  author can fix. The `&'static str` data model: yes, and it is the only
  conformance gap — §15.1 is unmeetable without it. `Measured`'s missing sign:
  no, and it rides along. The library-versus-binary panic asymmetry: **closed,
  not deferred** — the claim was wrong and 0.9.0 fixed the claim.

  If 2.0 happens, all of it happens at once: items 1 and 2 both change
  `ucal-core`'s public types, and a second major version for the leftovers would
  spend the same disruption twice for less.

- **[`X1-authoring-local-calendars.md`](../Proposals/X1-authoring-local-calendars.md)**
  — research into whether somebody who is not the author can derive a calendar
  for a body of their choosing. They cannot, and the survey turned up two
  defects that stand whether or not any of it is built.

  **§15.1 is unimplemented and was never recorded as such.** The specification
  requires an HJSON loader with body files and anchor files versioned
  independently; `data::all()` is a hardcoded `Vec` and `CALENDARS_WITH_ANCHORS`
  a hardcoded slice. Adding a body means editing the crate and republishing to
  crates.io — which is the difference between "a calendar can be derived" and
  "a calendar can be derived *by someone else*".

  `SPEC-DELTAS.md` records where the RFC is wrong and has no class for *the RFC
  is right and the implementation does not do it*, so eighteen deltas of
  bookkeeping never had anywhere to put this.

  The corroborating detail: **`UCAL-E0012` has no raiser anywhere in the
  workspace** — a diagnostic defined for a loader that does not exist. It was
  invisible until 1.2.0, when D-A19 moved `events show`'s misuse of it onto
  `UCAL-E0016`. Removing the one wrong caller left it with none, and a borrowed
  code stopped concealing a missing feature.

  **The leap rule is derived; the leap *placement* is not, and nothing declares
  it.** Rule K derives how many intercalary days a cycle holds. Which day is
  intercalated is a choice — `days_before_year` spreads them evenly, the
  Gregorian calendar clumps them at February — so a second conforming
  implementation could reproduce every vector, every convergent and every leap
  rule, and disagree with this one about a date. There is a one-line comment;
  there is no rule, no test against an alternative, and nothing in
  `spec/RULES.md`.

  The plan is four staged items, the first two being corrections rather than
  features, and each with a condition for stopping.

- **[`W4-two-ladders.md`](../Proposals/W4-two-ladders.md)** — a research
  proposal: two ladders side by side, universal on the left and one body's local
  calendar on the right, aligned, with zoom moving both.

  The research is not the view. It is the **local second**, which does not
  exist: a derived calendar has a year, a day, a day-fraction and cycles, and
  below the day there is nothing. Three routes are set out and the first is
  rejected on sight — dividing a body's day into 86 400 parts imports 24, 60 and
  60, which are Babylonian, and is failure mode F9 wearing a local name.

  The likely finding is written down before the work: **intercalation is
  derivable because two independent periods exist, and sub-day structure is not,
  because nothing below the day is a period of the body at all.** If that holds,
  the right-hand ladder stops at the day and the view must show it stopping
  rather than inventing rows to look symmetrical.

  Kill criterion first, as with GE-U4, and the same reason it is a proposal: it
  needs a reader the project does not have, and the cheap version comes first.

- **Step 1 of W4, run** — `crates/ucal-body/tests/ladder_alignment.rs`. Every
  unit of every shipped body placed on the universal ladder by exact rational
  arithmetic, in an afternoon, with no public API and no rendering.

  It found three things and then answered the proposal.

  **The arithmetic is right**: Earth's day places at 591.3 arcs against §4.3's
  published `1 d = 591.25 arc`, a figure nobody writing the probe chose.

  **Earth and Mars put their day on the same rung** — 591 and 607, on a ladder
  whose steps are a factor of 3125. Two planets, two separate measurements, and
  a grid built from powers of five with no knowledge of either.

  **Two independently derived quantities agree.** `earth-d`'s synodic month
  comes from `derive_cycles` expanding a continued fraction over two orbital
  periods; `luna-d`'s solar day comes from a published figure on a fact sheet.
  Different code, different data, same rung and the same residual to within a
  per cent. Found by running the probe, not by looking for it — which is the
  argument for placing things on a common scale, made by the probe itself.

  **And the finding that decides it: every unit of every body lands on `T1` or
  `T2`.** Fifteen units, seven calendars, two adjacent rungs out of forty-five.
  The right-hand column of the proposed view is not sparse, it is *degenerate* —
  forty-three rungs hold nothing for any body, ever, and zoom cannot help
  because zooming far enough to separate the two rungs leaves two rows and no
  ladder.

  **Recommendation recorded in the proposal: do not build steps 2 and 3.** What
  is worth keeping is the table the probe already prints. The proposal is left
  unedited above its outcome section, because one rewritten to agree with its
  own result records nothing.

## Changed

- **`ucal timeline` is no longer one screen, and the claim changed rather than
  the data.** §20 called it the one-screen demo; that was true at eleven events
  and is not at twenty-two, where the text runs to about a hundred and twenty
  lines.

  The obvious fix was to drop `as_published` from the timeline's rows. That
  would have removed a `ucal-json/1` path, and **promise 4 does not bend because
  a terminal rendering grew** — the fields a consumer reads are not free to move
  for the convenience of a display. So the help text and the doc comment say
  "in one document" instead, and the test asserts what one-screen was actually
  standing in for: that the whole of time is present, now including the far
  future.

## Fixed

- **`check-links` failed on a link to a file this cycle added.** A doc comment
  pointing at `blob/main/…/W4-two-ladders.md` 404s until the branch merges — and
  the release procedure runs the check *before* the merge, so every cycle that
  adds a document would hit it.

  The check now recognises the one signature that is always a false alarm: a
  404 on **this repository's own `main`** for a path that is **sitting in the
  working tree**. It reports the link as unmerged rather than dead and does not
  count it.

  Verified strict in both directions: a 404 on our own `main` for a file that is
  *not* here still fails.

- **`body_file` broke every build without the `body` feature.** The module loads
  a file that declares a `Body` and so needs `ucal-body`, and it was added
  ungated — so `ucal` compiled with `u512,std` alone stopped compiling at all.

  The `features` workflow caught it on the first push, which is the third time
  that workflow has caught something a full-featured build could not see. Gated,
  and all five `ucal` combinations build again.

- **The worked-examples check could cry wolf.** It runs whatever sits at
  `target/release/ucal` and has no idea whether that binary is current, so a
  tree whose sources have moved since the last release build produced a
  **failure with a correct committed file** — and a message that said "stale;
  regenerate", which is the wrong diagnosis and sends the reader to fix
  something that is not broken.

  It happened during this cycle and cost a few minutes of hunting a defect that
  was not there. CI never sees it, because every run builds from scratch, so it
  is a local-only trap.

  Not fixed by having the check build the binary: `check-docs` would go from
  milliseconds to minutes, and a check nobody runs because it is slow is worse
  than one that occasionally misreports. The message now names both
  possibilities and puts the likely one first, and the module records the trap.
  Verified by inducing a real mismatch and reading what comes out.

- **A published redshift had been glossed into Earth units.** Two of the new
  entries carried a parenthetical conversion — `z_eq = 3387 ± 21 (about 51 kyr)`
  and `z = 0.3 to 0.6 (about 10 Gyr)` — which the sources do not state and which
  put a Julian year into the default output of a non-Earth command.

  Caught by `no_earth_units.rs` before it left the branch, which is the check
  doing exactly what it was written for on the first data added since. The
  published figures stay redshifts; the conversion lives in the window, where it
  belongs.
